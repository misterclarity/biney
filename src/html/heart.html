Pair a heart-rate sensor

Watch your heart rate rise as you breathe in and fall as you breathe out. Any Bluetooth heart-rate sensor works: a Garmin watch set to broadcast, an ear-clip sensor or a chest strap.

Connection guide Step by step for a Garmin Venu 4, a HeartMath sensor or a chest strap, with a live check of each step
  1. Start broadcasting on the watch.Hold the upper-right button to open the controls menu and choose the heart-rate broadcast control. You can also go to Settings > Health & Wellness > Wrist Heart Rate > Broadcast Heart Rate. If the control isn't in the menu, you can add it when you customise the controls menu.
  2. Wear it snug and keep your arm still.Put it a finger's width above the wrist bone, tight enough not to slide. The optical sensor reads most steadily with your forearm resting on something.
  3. Connect here.Press Connect sensor. The watch appears in the list under its name within a few seconds; choose it and press Pair.
  4. If it stays on Connecting…Accept the pairing request if your phone shows one; on some phones it waits in the notifications. If there's none, Garmin Connect on the same phone may be holding on to the watch: in your phone's Settings, open Apps, then Garmin Connect, tap Force stop, and connect here again. Open Garmin Connect afterwards to sync as usual. Turning the phone's Bluetooth off and on, and restarting the broadcast on the watch, can help too. Within a minute at most, Entrain stops trying and says which step didn't finish; Cancel stops it sooner.
  5. Stop when you're done.Press the upper-right button on the watch to stop broadcasting, because it uses extra battery.

What you'll get: heart rate about once a second, the swing in each breath and the resonance test. Garmin watches don't broadcast beat-to-beat intervals, so RMSSD stays empty and coherence is marked as an estimate.

  1. Free it from the HeartMath app.The sensor connects to one device at a time. Close the HeartMath or Inner Balance app on your phone or tablet (swipe it away from the recent-apps screen), or switch that device's Bluetooth off for a moment.
  2. Turn it on.Press the button on the sensor once. A rapidly flashing blue light means it's ready to connect. A Coherence Plus sensor blinking white is still looking for the device it last used, so step 1 isn't done yet. If the battery light blinks red, charge it first.
  3. Clip it on.Clip the sensor to your earlobe and the cord's clip to your collar so it doesn't tug. If readings are patchy, rub the earlobe for a few seconds to warm it.
  4. Connect here.Press Connect sensor and choose it from the list; look for a name with HeartMath in it or the ID number printed on the back of the sensor. If it doesn't appear, press My sensor isn't listed, which shows every nearby device.

What you'll get: if your sensor sends beat-to-beat intervals, as Elite HRV's compatibility list suggests, everything works: RMSSD after about 30 seconds and the full coherence score after 64. HeartMath doesn't document use with other apps, so if RMSSD still says it needs beat-to-beat data after a minute, your model sends heart rate only.

    If something doesn't work
    The sensor isn't in the list

    Check that it's switched on and free. On a Garmin, broadcasting has to be running. A HeartMath sensor needs the fast blue flash and the HeartMath app closed. A chest strap needs wet electrodes and skin contact. Keep it within a metre or two of the phone or computer, and if it still doesn't show, press My sensor isn't listed.

    Chrome on Android finds nothing at all

    Bluetooth has to be on: swipe down from the top of the screen and tap Bluetooth. Chrome also needs permission to find nearby devices. On a Samsung Galaxy, open Settings > Apps > Chrome > Permissions > Nearby devices and choose Allow. On older Android versions the permission is called Location, and location services must be on.

    I paired the sensor in my phone's Bluetooth settings

    Unpair it. The page connects to the sensor directly, and a system pairing can keep it busy. On a Samsung Galaxy, go to Settings > Connections > Bluetooth, tap the gear next to the sensor and choose Unpair.

    The Connect button is greyed out

    This browser can't use Bluetooth. Open the page in Chrome or Edge on an Android phone or a computer. On a phone, Samsung Internet may not offer it, so use Chrome. On an iPhone, Safari and Chrome can't reach sensors; Bluefy, a browser built for Web Bluetooth, can.

    It connects, then drops

    Move the phone closer, charge the sensor, and keep Chrome in front with the screen on, because Android pauses pages in the background. The page tries to reconnect five times on its own before giving up.

    Heart rate works, but RMSSD stays empty

    The sensor sends heart rate only, which is true of every Garmin watch. The swing and the resonance test still work, and coherence is shown as an estimate. For RMSSD and full coherence, use the HeartMath sensor or a chest strap.

    The numbers jump around

    Optical sensors on the wrist or ear lose the pulse when you move or your skin is cold. Sit still, rest your arm, tighten the watch a notch, or warm your earlobe. For a chest strap, wet the electrodes again.

    The page says Bluetooth is blocked

    You're looking at a copy embedded inside another page, such as a preview, and those block Bluetooth. Open the page at its own web address and connect from there.
