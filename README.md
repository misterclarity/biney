# Entrain

Paced breathing, binaural beats and live heart-rate biofeedback in one offline web app. It covers guided practices and programmes, yoga and capoeira routines, tapping, a monitored power nap, programmes for a mindful meal, waking up, sport, creativity and mindful sex, nonviolent-communication reflection, and Healthy living's recipes, supplements, routines and light, with one search box across all of it and Learn.

There are five tabs: Today, Library, Breathe, Heart and Learn.

- **Today** has your favourites, what you did lately and the last seven days, from one history of everything you do, with the heart-rate change and coherence whenever a sensor was connected. It also has a suggestion for the time of day and the six goals. History, behind it, charts the last twelve weeks or months: minutes, the heart-rate change from start to end, and coherence.
- **Library** holds every practice, breathing pattern and track, each under one goal (Sleep, Calm, Focus, Energy, Recover or Reflect), with your own routines and breathing patterns first. The filters combine: one goal or several, one technique or several, and length, posture and needs, again one or several each. Within a row the choices add up, the rows narrow each other, each chip counts what it would show, and a line under them says what's chosen, with Clear all. Each track has a page of its own, like a practice, and so do the custom primer and the beat builder.
- **Breathe** is the breathing guide. Timings of your own can be saved under a name (up to twelve): each becomes a chip beside the presets, a row in the library, a search result and a star for Today, and can be updated, renamed or deleted, with an undo.
- **Heart** puts first what you need: connecting, the connection guide and the resonance test (with your last result and its pace) until a sensor is on; then your live numbers, the breath-by-breath chart, the test, the spectrum and breath from the microphone, with the guide at the bottom. Each number and chart has its explanation folded under it: what heart rate, the swing, RMSSD and coherence mean, what the bars show, what the resonance test finds and why, and how to read the spectrum (hertz into breaths a minute, the band coherence searches, the peak's window, the low- and high-frequency bands). The spectrum's dashed line is your breathing: the guide's pace during a session, or the pace the microphone hears, and a line under the chart says where your heart's rhythm peaks, your breathing pace and whether they match, or how to add your breathing when there's none. Breath from the microphone is an option: with echo cancelling, noise suppression and automatic gain off, it keeps the band where breath noise lies (0.5 to 5 kHz), measures its loudness ten times a second and finds the rhythm in the last minute (Nam et al. 2016's method), taking half that rhythm when in- and out-breath sound different and letting the guide's pace or the heart's own peak settle it when the sound alone can't. It shows a live trace of the breath sound with your heart rate under it, your pace (also beside the guide on Breathe), the swing of every breath it hears without a breathing session, how sure it is or what to change, a warning that Bluetooth earbuds turn mono while their microphone is in use (with a choice of microphone, kept), and what went wrong when the microphone is refused, missing or busy. The coherence score doesn't use it and stays HeartMath's, from the heartbeat alone. Nothing is recorded or sent: only the loudness of the last two minutes is kept, on the device, and the microphone stays on until Stop listening or the page closes. Coherence and the spectrum need the time between heartbeats, so a sensor that sends heart rate only, such as a Garmin watch, gets neither: a watch averages its heart rate over a few seconds, which flattens the rise and fall within each breath. With one, the resonance test says it leans towards slower paces, for the same reason, and so does a result it gave. The connection guide opens on the HeartMath sensor; its Garmin steps start with a device that isn't paired with the watch, since the watch answers nothing from the phone it syncs with in Garmin Connect. On an iPhone or iPad, where no browser offers Bluetooth to web pages, the page points to Bluefy, a browser built for it. Connecting can't get stuck: linking up, finding the heart-rate service and starting the readings each have a deadline (half a minute for the last two on the first try, time enough to confirm a pairing request on the watch or the phone, which the message suggests after a few seconds), and a step that fails or runs out of time is tried again, three tries in all. The later tries give the sensor a few seconds to settle once linked (6, then 12) and check that it answers a test read before asking for its readings, and a wait that worked is kept for that sensor's next first try. The button reads Cancel meanwhile, and the message says which step it's on, or which didn't finish and what to try (such as a pairing request to accept or, for a Garmin watch, Garmin Connect holding on to it), with a folded list of what happened on each try, word for word, and a button to copy it when asking for help.
- **Settings** holds the defaults; options can also be kept for each practice. It also decides how the screen behaves: by default it stays on and at full brightness through every session. Under Your data it saves a backup of everything as one file and restores one, for a new phone or for safekeeping. Its Feedback form (also reached at #feedback, from search and from the impressum) takes an idea, a problem, praise or a question, with a title and, if you like, the app's build and your browser, and opens it as a new issue on the project's GitHub page, ready to send there: the app itself sends nothing, and the draft stays on the device until it's cleared. About shows the build, a short id made from the sources.

Heart Coherence is three minutes of heart-focused breathing, then breathing with gratitude: the feeling comes second because it's easiest to feel once slow breathing has settled the heart rhythm, as it is after a meditation. It can be done sitting, lying down or walking, for 10, 15 or 20 minutes, over a theta, alpha or alpha-to-theta beat or none, at its optimal default (your resonance rate: the test's answer, or 5.5 a minute until you take it) or at an in and out of your own. Those choices are kept for it, like its other options.

Gratitude is a practice of its own: three or four minutes of slow, even breathing at the same pace choices first, because gratitude is easiest to feel once you are calm, then gratitude for something small from today, for a person who helped you and for your own body, a minute of savouring the feeling, and an invitation to write down three good things. It runs 10 or 15 minutes, sitting, lying down or walking. Calm Reset has the same beat and pace choices as Heart Coherence: its usual alpha-to-theta slide, a steady theta or alpha beat, or none, and the optimal default pace or an in and out of your own for its resonance breathing (the cyclic sighs keep their rhythm), kept for it.

For a hard conversation or a stressful meeting there are four practices. Difficult Conversation (6, 10 or 15 minutes) settles the body first: cyclic sighs, then slow breathing at your resonance rate with a longer out-breath (40% in, 60% out), with live coherence from a sensor and a beat that slides from 10 Hz alpha into 6 Hz theta for the breathing and climbs back to alpha for the last steps, so you go in calm and awake. Four short steps follow: reading a racing heart as readiness, a value of your own, your first sentence, and an if-then plan said to yourself by name. It can begin with an NVC practice, chosen on its page under Begin with: Prepare a Hard Conversation (NVC), written for it, which asks what happened, what makes the conversation hard for you, your feelings and needs, a guess at theirs and a request, then drafts a first sentence from your answers for you to make your own; or Self-Empathy. That practice's end screen leads straight on, and Difficult Conversation brings back the needs, request and first sentence, from the same session or from a preparation kept in the last twelve hours. During the Call runs beside a video call until you tap The call's over: a 10 Hz beat (16 Hz for long meetings) at half your usual volume on a low 200 Hz carrier, a silent pacer to glance at while you listen, or a reminder every five minutes, and your heart rate and coherence. It starts silent, with no voice, bells, breath tones or vibration, and the call itself stays silent even with the voice on. After the Conversation lets the stress settle: sighs and long out-breaths under a theta beat, naming the feeling, kindness to yourself, what went well and one next step. These defaults are the practices' own, set over the ones for every practice and changeable like any option; Anapana reminders start off in all three. Each end screen suggests the one that comes next.

Autogenic Training is Schultz's standard method, the one NASA adapted for astronauts as Autogenic-Feedback Training. A short spell of paced breathing with a longer out-breath comes first, as in NASA's version. Then six exercises, each a short phrase said silently while the voice says it softly with you and the body map lights the part it names: heaviness and warmth limb by limb, a calm and regular heartbeat, the breath that breathes itself, a warm solar plexus and a cool forehead, with "I am completely calm" after each, then all of them together, and back with arms firm, a deep breath and eyes open. Each phrase comes back every twenty seconds or so, to repeat silently in between. Its choices: lying down, an armchair or the coachman's seat; all six, or the week of the classic course you're in (each week adds one exercise and gives it the most time); the heartbeat phrase, a warm chest, or neither; and back to alert, or into sleep. It runs 5 to 20 minutes, with a binaural beat if you add one and a heart sensor as the feedback. Autogenic Reframing turns it to anxious moments: naming what your body is doing, the reminder that anxiety and excitement are nearly identical in the body, cyclic sighs and long out-breaths, short autogenic phrases with the heart phrase rewritten to welcome a strong heartbeat as readiness, then "I am excited" (or "I'm ready for this", or "This energy helps me") to answer the anxious thought, said again as its intention formula. Each suggests the other at its end. A practice that ends in sleep (Autogenic Training's bedtime ending, Sleep Descent, the Cognitive Shuffle) ends without the end bells.

Mindful Meal is for eating a meal slowly and attentively, at breakfast, lunch or dinner, in 20, 30 or 45 minutes. Before the first bite: a moment to arrive, your hunger rated from 0 to 10, a minute and a half of slow breathing at your resonance pace with a longer out-breath (or a minute of cyclic sighs), a moment of gratitude for the meal and the people who brought it to you, then the food looked at and smelt and the first two bites taken slowly, noticing taste, texture and temperature. The rest of the meal runs at your own pace with the screen dimmed, until you tap I've finished or the time you chose runs out, with a reminder to pause every two and a half minutes or so; halfway through, one asks about your hunger. Then your hunger again, set beside the first on the end screen and in the history, gratitude if you chose it for after, and a breath before you get up. Every part is a choice on its page, kept for it, with the usual one chosen for you: the hunger check, before and after or left out; slow breathing, cyclic sighs or straight to the meal, at the optimal default pace or your own; gratitude before, after, both or not at all; the guided first bites, or not; reminders spoken softly, as a soft chime with a few words on the screen (it sounds even with the bells off), or silence; and a steady 10 Hz alpha beat at half your usual volume, a 6 Hz theta beat, or none, for a meal with others. The other options are there as for any practice; Anapana reminders start off, and tapping isn't offered. The end screen suggests a short walk, since light walking after a meal lowers the rise in blood sugar. Each dish's recipe ends with a link to it, and so do breakfast, lunch and the evening meal in the routines.

Morning Tonic is Sleep Descent the other way round, for 10, 15 or 20 minutes on waking: a long stretch in bed (or sitting up), then breathing that starts slow, with the out-breath longer, evens out and quickens, the in-breath leading, while a binaural beat rises from 6 Hz theta through 10 Hz alpha to 16 Hz beta (or only to alpha); two rounds of breath of fire if you choose it; stretches in bed or seated and a few standing poses with drawings, or the breathing only; then daylight, a glass of water and one thing you'd like from today. It's Today's suggestion early in the morning, and the morning routine links to it.

Sports Performance is for cycling, running, rowing or weight training. Each sport brings its own settings, which you can change, and picking a sport again brings them back; the options that don't apply (the cadence tick for weights, the sets and rests for the others) stay out of the way. Before a session, in 5 or 10 minutes: slow breathing at your resonance pace (for weight training, power breaths and the bracing breath), a rehearsal of the session in your mind, and a few words of your own for when it gets hard, while the beat rises from 10 Hz alpha to 15 Hz beta (a steady 15 Hz for weight training). During, if you choose it: an endurance session runs until you tap Session over, at most two hours, with a cue every five minutes or so (technique, breathing and your words) and a soft tick at your cadence (on for running, at 170 steps a minute, or slower or quicker); weight training goes set by set, tapping Set done after each and resting with slow breathing for the time chosen. A cool-down follows, and the end screen suggests Exercise Recovery.

Creative Flow, in 15, 20 or 30 minutes: slow breathing to ease in, open awareness, a game to loosen up (unusual uses for everyday things, or random words to link to your question), ideas for your own question, then a daydream, an easy walk or Edison's twilight (dozing with a spoon in your hand) to let them brew, and time to capture them; or straight on into the work until you tap I'm done. Its beat is 10 Hz alpha, dipping to 6 Hz theta while ideas brew.

Mindful Sex, in 20, 30 or 45 minutes, with a partner or on your own: arriving and, with a partner, agreeing what you'd love and that either of you can pause at any time; slow breathing together; soft eye contact; slow touch in turns (sensate focus); then lovemaking at your own pace with the screen dimmed and a reminder every four minutes or so to come back to breath and sensation (a soft chime, spoken softly, or silence) until you tap that you're done; and afterglow. Choose slow and present, with a longer out-breath under a 6 Hz theta beat, or building intensity, with fuller breaths under a 10 Hz alpha beat. Learn sets out what the research shows about that choice, and what it doesn't: no study has tested binaural beats during sex.

Nine practices can also be done walking, chosen under Position: Calm Reset, Heart Coherence, Gratitude, Difficult Conversation (on the way there), After the Conversation, Autogenic Reframing, Loving-Kindness (which then includes the people you pass), 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding and Exercise Recovery (as a cool-down walk). Walking, the guidance talks about the path and your steps, a few to each in-breath and a few more to each out-breath, rather than a chair, and the choice asks for a quiet route away from traffic with the volume low enough to hear what's around you. The library's Walking filter lists them with the Walking Meditation.

While a practice runs, its breathing can be made slower or faster, from the next breath, and its beat lower or higher, gliding there over a second and a half, or turned off and on again. Restart starts it again from the top with two taps, like End, keeping those changes; the reflections leave it out, since it would lose what you've written.

A practice's end screen sets each number against your usual for that practice: the middle of your last ten sessions with the same kind of reading, or last time if there was only one, and says when it's your best yet.

The safety note is written once, in Learn under Safety, and opens over any page from a "safety notes" link. Each page shows only the line that matters there: the beats on a track, the primer, the builder and any practice that plays one; the breath on Breathe; a practice's own cautions in its introduction.

A playing track shows on the phone's lock screen and in its notifications, with play, pause and stop; Settings can turn that off. The breathing guide, and a practice's breath steps, can vibrate at each change: one short buzz in, one long out, two light taps for a hold.

Healthy living, opened from the top of Learn and from Today, has four tabs. Recipes: nine simple dishes (congee, kitchari, stewed apple with oats, miso soup, red lentil dal, overnight oats, a salmon traybake, a chickpea stew and golden milk), each on a page of its own where the servings, from 1 to 12, scale every amount, the ingredients and steps can be ticked off as you go, and the screen can be kept on while you cook; a dish's page ends with the Mindful Meal, for eating it slowly. A recipe can be starred for Today. Supplements: omega-3, coenzyme Q10, creatine, vitamin D, magnesium and vitamin B12 in full, and seven more in brief, each a card that opens to what it may help with the strength of the evidence for each, the amounts studied, how to take it, food sources, what to check first and its sources; and coffee, in a section of its own, whose card explains what a cup does in the body part by part (how caffeine works, hour by hour with a chart of how long it stays, the brain, sleep, heart and blood pressure, heart readings, the gut, fluids, cholesterol, iron, and stopping), what it may help, how much is safe, the caffeine in a drink, when to have it, what to check first and its 24 sources; #living/coffee opens it. The safety note has a line for them, shown at the top of the tab. Routines: healthy sleep habits (regular hours, bright days and dim evenings, caffeine at least nine hours before bed, alcohol, exercise, a bed for sleep, short early naps, a wind-down, and CBT-I for insomnia that lasts), a morning routine (daylight in the first hour, a practice before the screens, breakfast sitting down and away from the screens), the day (short breaks from devices and screens, which let the body remember the feeling from the morning's practice and regulate around it; for analysis paralysis, a streak of intellectual overstimulation, the way out through affect: feeling, emotion or mood and the body's sensations at rest, observed consciously and lovingly; and lunch away from the screen) and an evening routine counted back from bedtime; a step's practices, recipes and pages wait behind a button named for them (Suggested practices, Suggested recipes, See also) that opens to show them, and each routine lists its sources. Today's suggestion links to the routine for the time of day, and #living/morning, #living/daytime, #living/evening and #living/sleep-habits open one. Light: a light box (sun lamp) and red and near-infrared light, as cards like the supplements', with a line of their own in the safety note.

The end screen's quote can be read aloud: the voice says the quote alone, and the author stays on the screen.

The impressum, copyright and licence are at the end of Learn, linked from the end of Settings. A button at the end of Today, of Settings and of the end screen after every practice opens the donations page on Ko-fi (ko-fi.com/misterclarity) in the browser.

Updates install themselves. The app looks for a new version each time it opens, when it comes back to the screen after half an hour away, and every hour it stays on screen; a new version downloads in the background. It then installs (the page reloads into it) at a moment that interrupts nothing: straight away if the app hasn't been touched since it opened, otherwise the next time you leave it, once no practice, breathing session, resonance test or track is under way, no heart sensor is connected, the microphone isn't listening for your breathing, nothing is being typed and no routine or recipe is half done. Until then a notice offers Update now, and after an automatic update a short one says it happened.

Everything runs on the device and stays there: the history, favourites and reflections are kept in the browser's storage, and nothing is sent anywhere. Once there is a session worth keeping, the app asks the browser to keep its storage when space runs low, and Settings says what the browser answered.

The breathing guide and the practices' visuals draw 30 frames a second, even on a faster screen, and the glows are gradients rather than blurred shadows, which are slow to redraw. The player's clock ticks once a second. The app opens without building the library, which is made each time you open it; its practices' lengths are worked out while the phone is idle after start-up.

## Build

```
python3 src/build.py
```

This writes `index.html`, `sw.js` and `manifest.webmanifest` at the top level. It also writes `entrain-page.html`, a preview page without the offline helper, which is not committed. A framed page can't download files itself, so in that preview Save a backup goes through the viewer's `downloads` capability, which the artifact has to declare.

The build only assembles, nothing is rewritten on the way:

- **Markup.** Each `{include:name}` line in `src/html/app.html` is replaced by `src/html/name`, and `{i:name}` anywhere in the markup becomes an icon from `src/html/icons.svg`. The build stops on an icon that isn't in the sprite.
- **Script.** The files in `src/js/` are joined in the order listed in `build.py`, inside one function, so they share one scope. `core.js` comes first and `boot.js` last. Every other file only declares things; `boot.js` wires the page up in order. Because the scope is shared, the build stops if a top-level name is declared twice. Otherwise the second declaration would silently replace the first.
- **Styles.** `src/styles.css`, with the Poppins subset from `src/poppins.b64` inlined and its licence, `src/poppins-OFL.txt`, in a comment just above it (the font's licence asks that every copy carry it).

## Test

You need Node 18 or later, Playwright with Chromium (`npm install playwright`, then `npx playwright install chromium`) and Python 3.

```
node tests/run.js              # every suite, as many at a time as there are cores (up to four)
node tests/run.js --jobs 1     # one at a time, about twenty minutes (two at a time takes ten)
node tests/run.js apptest      # one or more suites by name
```

See `tests/README.md` for what each suite covers.

## Publishing

`.github/workflows/test-and-publish.yml` runs on every push to the default branch. It checks that the committed `index.html`, `sw.js` and manifest are what `src/build.py` makes from the source, runs every suite, and publishes the site to GitHub Pages only if they all pass. For that, the repository's Settings > Pages > Build and deployment > Source has to be **GitHub Actions**. While it is still "Deploy from a branch", GitHub publishes every push straight from the branch, tested or not, and the workflow only runs the tests and says so in its summary.
