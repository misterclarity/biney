Tones your brain builds itself.

Every track here is generated live in your browser from two pure sine tones — one per ear. The beat you hear is the difference between them, and it exists only in your auditory brainstem. Frequencies, carrier tones and session lengths are taken from the published literature, and each card shows what the evidence actually says.

🎧 Headphones are mandatory. On speakers the two tones mix in the air and you get an ordinary acoustic beat, not a binaural one.
🔉 Keep it quiet. Beats are perceived most clearly at low, comfortable levels. Loud does not mean stronger.

    What the research actually supports

    The honest summary: real but modest effects, decent evidence for anxiety and attention, weaker and noisier evidence for memory.

    How a binaural beat is made

    Feed 250 Hz to the left ear and 260 Hz to the right, and neither ear receives a 10 Hz signal — yet you perceive a 10 Hz pulsing. The beat is produced by binaural interaction in the superior olivary complex of the brainstem, which is why the effect vanishes the moment the tones reach both ears (Oster, 1973). Two constraints follow from the physiology:

    • Carrier matters. The lower of the two tones is the carrier, and lower carriers produce a more robust percept. A 40 Hz auditory steady-state response has been evoked with a 400 Hz carrier but was undetectable above roughly 3000 Hz.
    • The beat has a ceiling. Beyond about a 35 Hz difference, the two tones separate perceptually and the beat disappears — which is why gamma tracks sit at the edge of what this method can do.

    Dosing: timing beats frequency-picking

    The 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies (35 effect sizes) found an overall medium effect, g = 0.45, and two practical findings that matter more than which exact frequency you choose:

    • Listen before the task, not only during it. Exposure before, or before and during, a task outperformed exposure during the task alone.
    • Longer is better. Time under exposure contributed significantly to the model. Most positive trials ran 15–30 minutes.
    • Masking is optional. White or pink noise over the top gave similar effects to unmasked beats — so the noise bed in the player is for comfort, not potency.

    Where the evidence is strongest, and weakest

    Anxiety is the best-supported outcome: theta/delta beats showed a medium-to-large effect, g = 0.69. Attention and vigilance hold up reasonably well. Memory is genuinely contested — a 2022 meta-analysis of 15 studies reported a medium overall effect, but the accompanying systematic review found conflicting results for theta and beta on memory tasks, and several 40 Hz studies found no entrainment at all with their parameters. Individual differences are real: alpha beats improved divergent thinking only in participants with lower estimated baseline dopamine.

    Treat the strength bars on each card as what they are — a rough read of how consistent the literature is for that band, not a promise about your session.

    The tinnitus track works differently

    Everything else on this page rests on entrainment — a beat frequency nudging cortical rhythms. Notched Relief does not. Its active ingredient is the absence of sound: removing a one-octave band centred on your own tinnitus pitch is thought to reduce the over-excitable auditory cortex neurons tuned to that frequency, through lateral inhibition from their unmasked neighbours. That is why it needs a pitch match and why the notch is useless if it is aimed at the wrong frequency. The 8 Hz alpha beat rides on top because adding one to relaxing sound improved tinnitus handicap and emotional scores more than the sound alone.

    Two caveats worth stating plainly. The notched-training results held for tinnitus at or below 8 kHz and were not observed above it. And the timescale is not one session: the trials ran 15 minutes twice daily for four weeks, or daily listening across twelve months for the roughly 30% loudness reduction. Sound therapy is an adjunct to clinical care, and for tinnitus distress specifically, cognitive behavioural therapy remains the better-supported intervention.

    Breathing runs on a slower clock

    The breathing guide works through a different route from the beats. Each breath nudges heart rate up on the way in and down on the way out. At around six breaths a minute, a rhythm of 0.1 Hz, that swing falls into step with the baroreflex, the loop that steadies blood pressure, and the two reinforce each other. Heart-rate variability peaks there, which is why HRV biofeedback trains people to breathe at that pace (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014). A 2022 meta-analysis of 223 studies found that voluntary slow breathing raised vagally mediated heart-rate variability during a session, straight after one, and after multi-session training.

    The mood findings are more modest. A 2023 meta-analysis of randomised trials found breathwork lowered self-reported stress by a small-to-medium amount (g = −0.35), with similar effects on anxiety and depression, but most of the trials carried a moderate risk of bias. The trial behind cyclic sighing is randomised but small and was run remotely. As with the beats, read the strength bars as a summary of the literature, not a promise about your session.

    Quick headphone check

    A binaural beat only exists if each ear receives its own tone. On speakers, a single earbud, or a mono Bluetooth device the two tones mix before they reach you and nothing on this page does anything. This takes five seconds.
